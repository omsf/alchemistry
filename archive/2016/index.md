# Description
In May 2016 two back-to-back workshops were held in the Boston area. The **2016 Workshop on Free Energy Methods in Drug Design: Targeting Cancer** took place May 16th-18th, 2016 in the Auditorium of Building 1 at Vertex Pharmaceuticals Headquarters, 50 Northern Avenue, Boston, MA. It focused on free energy techniques&mdash;the latest innovations and use cases, force field parameterization as it touches these techniques, and sampling practices&mdash;with the goal of bringing together experts from pharma, supporting industries and academia to assess where the field stands, identify the challenges that must be tackled next to bring free energy techniques to bear on real-world applications, and identify particular problems in drug discovery where these methods can have an impact. Participants were encouraged to discuss pitfalls and failures as well as successes.

It was followed by the **2016 Workshop on Kinetics and Markov State Models in Drug Design**, held May 19th-20th, 2016 at the Novartis Institutes for BioMedical Research, 220 Massachusetts Ave., Cambridge, MA. This event focused on the study of the kinetics of biomolecular processes at the atomistic and systems scales via *in-silico* approaches such as Markov State Models (MSMs) and other suitable techniques, including the use of MSMs in the study of binding kinetics, allostery and conformational transitions, stochastic algorithms for the kinetics of non-equilibrium bioprocesses at the systems level, and the role of these approaches in improving the accuracy and efficiency of drug discovery.

# Workshop on Free Energy Methods in Drug Design: Targeting Cancer (May 16–18, Vertex)

## Organization Committee

Previous organizers and current advisors:
* David Mobley — University of California, Irvine
* Vijay Pande — Stanford University

* Camilo Velez-Vega — Entasis Therapeutics

## Themes
1. Application of Free Energy Methods to New and Established Cancer Therapeutics
2. Optimization for Potency and/or Selectivity
3. Sensitivity of Binding Results to Force Field, Sampling and Simulation Choices
4. Constant pH and Chemical Effects (i.e. Protomers and Tautomers) During Binding
5. Beyond Binding: Free Energy Calculations Throughout the Drug Discovery Pipeline
6. Predicting Resistance and Designing Biologics: Protein Mutational Free Energies
7. New or Enhanced Tools to Setup and Execute Successful Free Energy Calculations
