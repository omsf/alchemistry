# Description
The 2014 workshop titled **2014 Workshop on the State of Free Energy Calculations in Drug Design** was held May 19-21st, 2014, in the auditorium of Building 1 at Vertex Pharmaceuticals headquarters (Fan Pier, 50 Northern Avenue) in Boston, MA. The workshop focused on free energy techniques&mdash;the latest innovations and use cases, force field parameterization as it touches these techniques, and sampling practices, among other things. The organizers believed these techniques were closer than ever to making an impact in appropriate drug discovery applications by allowing physics-based prediction of physical properties such as binding affinity, selectivity, solubility, and membrane permeability, while recognizing that a number of challenges remained. The workshop aimed to assess where the field stood, what challenges must be tackled next to bring free energy techniques to bear on real-world applications, and which problems in drug discovery these methods could impact.

The goal was to bring together experts from pharma and supporting industries, as well as academia, in an intense and focused workshop to identify challenges and help chart the path forward, with particular interest in use cases, pitfalls and their solutions; participants were encouraged to go beyond success stories and provide detailed insight into both successes and failures. Talks spanned three days with ample time for discussion, around themes including: use cases for free energy techniques in real-world problems where they can or have had an impact; how pharma can best benefit today or in the immediate future; parameterization of small molecules and proteins and how the latest innovations affect free energies; good sampling practices and the challenges facing sampling; and the design of experimental and simulation studies that could directly demonstrate the utility of free energy calculations in widespread industrial use. Unlike previous editions held at Vertex's Cambridge site, the 2014 meeting moved to the Boston auditorium, which allowed roughly 90 participants and required no confidentiality agreements, and the organizers made a bigger effort to increase industry participation, aiming for at least one third of the talks to come from industrial researchers.
